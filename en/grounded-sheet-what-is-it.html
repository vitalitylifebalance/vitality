Grounded sheet: what it is, how it works and how to choose one

24 September 2026 Read time: 14 min Vitality Life Balance

Grounded sheet is the plain name for a grounding or earthing sheet: a conductive fabric connected to the earth pin of a socket.

Direct answer

A grounded sheet (also called an earthing sheet, grounding sheet or earthed bed sheet) is a fitted sheet with conductive thread connected by a cable to the earth pin of a Schuko socket. It doesn't use mains electricity, it doesn't heat up and it doesn't block radiation: it simply connects your skin to the electrical potential of the earth.

In short
  • It's the same product as a "grounding sheet" or "earthing sheet": the name changes from shop to shop, the object doesn't.
  • It has conductive thread (silver, in Vitality's case) connected by a snap button to a cable with a safety resistor.
  • The cable connects only to the socket's earth pin, never to the live poles: it doesn't heat up or carry mains electricity.
  • It isn't an electric blanket or an "anti-radiation" fabric: those are different product categories.
  • The available scientific evidence is preliminary and based on small samples; it doesn't promise to cure or treat anything.

Why does the grounded sheet have so many names?

Direct answer: because every sales channel translates it into its own vocabulary: general retail calls it "earthed" (an electrician's word), EMF wellness shops "earth-connected" (a nod to the socket), foreign brands with localised websites "grounded", and the biohacking niche "grounding" or "earthing". It's the same product in all four cases.

If you've come across this piece of bedding in very different kinds of shop, that's no coincidence. In Spain, a general DIY retailer such as Leroy Merlin sells it as «Sábanas de Puesta a Tierra» — literally "earthed sheets" — using the same vocabulary it would use for an installation cable. A shop specialising in electromagnetic protection will list it as an "earthing or grounding sheet, for earth connection". A foreign brand with a localised website will call it an "earth-connected sheet", and in Spanish-speaking markets (including some shops in Mexico and Colombia) you'll also see «sábana de conexión a tierra» or «sábana con toma de tierra». And a biohacking blog will simply talk about a "grounding sheet", borrowing the niche's own term.

Grounding and earthing are the niche's two English words; we explain their nuances in the difference between grounding and earthing (in Spanish). Here all that matters is that both refer to this same sheet.

How does an earthing sheet work, part by part?

Direct answer: a grounded sheet has conductive thread woven into the fabric, a snap button that connects it to a cable with a safety resistor, and an adapter that engages only the socket's earth pin. Without a socket with a working earth, the circuit stays open and the sheet doesn't conduct.

The mechanism in the body is covered in what grounding is and how it works in the body (in Spanish); here, the four physical parts of this sheet:

  1. The conductive fabric: conductive thread is woven in a grid across the whole contact surface, on a cotton base. Vitality uses 5% silver thread (10% variant available).
  2. The snap button: in one corner of the sheet, it's the fabric's electrical exit point, where the cable clips on.
  3. The cable with a safety resistor: it links the snap button to the adapter, and its built-in resistor limits the current that could reach the fabric if the socket were faulty.
  4. The plug adapter: it uses only the Schuko's earth pin; by design, it doesn't connect to the home's live poles. If the socket's earth had a fault, the cable's resistor limits the current.

With those parts in place and a socket with a real earth, the sheet sits at the same electrical potential as the rest of the building's installation; without a working earth, the circuit stays open and there's no connection, however well the other parts are put together. To check your socket and plug the sheet in, follow how to connect it to a Schuko socket, step by step.

On the underlying physics: an electrical engineering team from outside the industry, Chamberlin et al. (2014), measured the actual current between a person and the earth connection: in the order of nanoamps, a current the authors themselves describe as minuscule (DOI 10.1016/j.jcm.2014.10.001). There's no mains current: just a tiny potential-equalising current.

What isn't an earth-connected sheet?

Direct answer: it isn't an electric blanket (it doesn't heat up or carry mains current), it isn't an electromagnetic shielding fabric (it doesn't block or attenuate radio-frequency waves: that's another product category) and it doesn't put mains current into your body. Its only function is to connect your skin to earth potential while you're touching it.

The confusion with electric blankets is understandable, since both are bedding with a cable running to a socket. But a grounded sheet has no heating element and no temperature control, and its cable isn't connected to the live poles, only to the earth pin; if the socket develops a fault, the safety resistor limits the current that could reach the fabric. If you're after warmth, that's a different product.

The confusion with "anti-radiation" or electromagnetic shielding fabric also comes up often, because some EMF shops sell both products side by side. They're different categories: a shielding fabric is designed to attenuate electromagnetic fields in the environment, whereas a grounded sheet is designed to conduct earth potential to your skin.

Is a grounded sheet safe? (the essentials)

Direct answer: the resistor built into the cable limits the current that could reach the fabric if the socket had an electrical fault; it doesn't eliminate it, it limits it. The Vitality Grounding Sheet has a resistance of ~100 kΩ — our own measurement, not an external certification; other manufacturers state between ~50 and 100 kΩ on their spec sheets, also without external verification.

For most users, with a socket in good condition, the cable's resistor is the key safety measure. How to choose a grounded sheet: what to check before you buy

Direct answer: before buying, check six aspects of the product, not just the price: the conductive thread's material, the declared percentage and the documents behind it, the cable's safety resistor, whether testers are included, the right size and format for your bed, and clear washing instructions. Each one determines whether you'll actually be able to use it and check that it works.

  1. Conductive thread material. Silver and stainless steel are the two usual materials in the industry. Silver, as a material, conducts far better from the first use: according to standard electrical conductivity values, it's around ~63×10⁶ S/m versus ~1.4×10⁶ S/m for 316L stainless steel — figures for the material itself, not for the finished fabric. We cover this comparison, with its durability nuances, in why silver conducts better than steel.
  2. Declared percentage, and whether a document backs it up. A silver percentage (or one for any other conductive material) without a spec sheet to go with it is a figure you can't verify. We don't compare other brands' percentages here: we simply point out that if a listing doesn't state the percentage or reference a spec sheet, that's a reasonable warning sign.
  3. Cable with a declared safety resistor. It should appear on the product spec sheet, with its approximate value, and it should be clear whether it's the manufacturer's own measurement or a third-party certification — they're different things, and the shop shouldn't blur them.
  4. Testers included. Ideally two: one to check that the socket has a working earth, and another to check the electrical continuity of the fabric itself. Without them, you have no easy way of knowing whether what you've bought actually conducts.
  5. The right size and format. Grounded fitted sheets usually come in single (for 90 cm beds) or double (for 135-150 cm beds). It also matters that the design gives you real contact with your skin while you sleep, not just a decorative border with conductive thread.
  6. Clear washing instructions. Conductive thread can degrade if washed incorrectly; a serious spec sheet should explain, at the very least, whether it's machine-washable, at what temperature and which products to avoid.

These six points are the anatomy of the product itself. How do you wash it so it keeps conducting?

Direct answer: cold, or at 30 °C at most, with no fabric softener or bleach, and air-dried: those are the basic rules. Fabric softener leaves a film on the conductive thread that is the most common cause of conductivity loss over time; very hot water can also damage it.

The limits of the evidence: what has been measured and what hasn't

Direct answer: if you're wondering whether a grounded sheet works, the honest approach is to separate what's been measured from what's been promised. Several small studies have measured night-time cortisol, the body's electrical potential or physiological parameters — never on this particular sheet — with small samples and no large-scale blinded design: the evidence is preliminary and no study allows results to be promised.

Ghaly and Teplitz (2004) measured night-time cortisol levels and what participants reported about their sleep, pain and stress, in 12 people sleeping grounded compared with their own baseline. It's an exploratory study, without a blinded control group (Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine, DOI 10.1089/acm.2004.10.767).

Sokal and Sokal (2012) measured the electrical potential of the human body before and after grounding it in a small group of subjects, and recorded a drop of around 200 mV; it's an electrical measurement, not a health outcome, and it was carried out with few participants (Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine, DOI 10.1089/acm.2010.0683).

Chevalier et al. (2012) published a narrative review bringing together several earlier studies on grounding and proposing electron transfer from the Earth's surface as a physiological mechanism; its evidence is preliminary, not a settled conclusion (Journal of Environmental and Public Health, DOI 10.1155/2012/291541).

Müller et al. (2019) designed a more careful study, with grounded sleep after intense eccentric muscle exercise, and found no clear effects on most of the variables measured (Frontiers in Physiology, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2019.00035) — a result just as worth mentioning as the others.

None of these studies assessed a specific brand's grounded sheet, and none replaces the judgement of a healthcare professional: the sheet is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition. You can see the full list of published studies, each with its limitation flagged, in published studies, with DOI (in Spanish); and if your core question is whether grounding "works" or not, we answer it in more depth in do grounding sheets work?

Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

What is a grounded sheet?

It's a fitted sheet with conductive thread connected by a cable to the earth pin of a Schuko socket. It doesn't use mains electricity: it simply puts your skin in contact with the electrical potential of the earth, without heating or blocking anything.

Is a grounded sheet the same as a grounding sheet or an earthing sheet?

Yes, it's the same product. "Grounding" and "earthing" are the terms the wellness niche uses; "grounded sheet" or "earthed sheet" is the plain-language version used by general retailers and by people searching for the product without knowing the industry jargon. There's no difference in parts or in how they work.

Why do some shops call it an "earthed sheet" and others a "grounding sheet"?

Because each shop adapts the name to its audience. DIY retailers use an electrician's vocabulary ("earthed"), while wellness shops use the industry term ("grounding"). The object they sell is identical in both cases: a fitted sheet with conductive thread and a cable to earth.

Are an "earth-connected sheet" and a "grounded sheet" different things?

No, they're two ways of naming the same product. "Earth-connected" puts the focus on the socket it plugs into; "grounded" puts the focus on the result of that connection. Before buying either, check that your socket has a working earth: without it, no sheet sold under either name can conduct.

Is a grounded sheet an electric blanket, or does it protect against radiation?

It's neither. It doesn't heat up or carry mains current like an electric blanket, and it doesn't block or attenuate electromagnetic fields like a shielding fabric: they're different product categories. Its only function is to connect your skin to the electrical potential of the earth through the socket's earth pin.
